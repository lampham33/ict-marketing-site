ICT order block strategy guide

This ICT order block strategy guide shows how traders combine order blocks, session timing, and liquidity into one written plan. It is a teaching example, not a recommendation.

How to identify ICT order blocks

An order block is the last opposing candle before a strong move. A bullish order block is the last down-closing candle before a strong move up. A bearish order block is the last up-closing candle before a strong move down. Use these checks to decide whether a block is worth marking, starting on a higher time frame such as the 4-hour or daily chart.

CheckWhat to look for
Opposing candleThe candle closes against the direction of the move that follows
DisplacementA strong, fast move away from the candle, usually with large bodies
Structure breakThe move breaks a recent swing high or low
ImbalanceThe move often leaves a fair value gap behind it
UntestedPrice has not yet returned to the block

ICT killzones

ICT killzones are time windows when traders expect more orders and larger moves. Exact hours vary by teacher, so treat the times below as common starting points in New York time and check them against your own broker and chart settings.

SessionCommon window (Eastern)
Asia8:00 PM to 12:00 AM
London2:00 AM to 5:00 AM
New York open7:00 AM to 10:00 AM
London close10:00 AM to 12:00 PM

10 AM open market liquidity

US stock index futures begin the regular session at 9:30 AM Eastern. Many ICT students then study the window around 10:00 AM Eastern, where 10 am open market liquidity is often discussed. The idea is that price may sweep a nearby high or low, taking resting orders, before choosing a direction. Always convert times to Eastern Time before you mark your chart.

Step-by-step plan

  1. Mark liquidity. Draw lines at the previous session high and low and at recent swing points.
  2. Pick a kill zone. Trade only inside a window you have chosen in advance.
  3. Look for a sweep. Around 10:00 AM, see whether price takes a marked high or low and then reverses.
  4. Confirm with displacement. Look for a strong, fast move away from the level, often leaving a fair value gap.
  5. Mark the order block. Find the last opposing candle before that move and run the checks above.
  6. Plan the entry. Wait for price to return to the order block or gap.
  7. Set risk. Place the stop beyond the sweep and risk a small, fixed share of your account.
  8. Choose targets. Aim for the opposite liquidity pool, such as the other session extreme.
