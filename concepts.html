Fair value gaps, inversions, and rejection blocks

These ideas describe how traders read imbalances and reactions on a price chart. Each section gives a plain definition, how to mark it, and a common way it is used.

Fair value gap trading

A fair value gap (FVG) is a three-candle pattern in which the first candle and third candle do not overlap, leaving a gap across the middle candle. In a bullish FVG, the gap sits between the high of candle 1 and the low of candle 3. Traders view it as an imbalance where price moved too quickly for both sides to trade fairly.

In fair value gap trading, the common idea is that price may return to the gap and react there before continuing in its original direction.

Simplified diagram: the shaded band is the bullish fair value gap, running from the top of candle 1 to the bottom of candle 3.

ICT fair value gap trading strategy: retest steps

  1. Find direction. Check a higher time frame to see whether price is trending up or down.
  2. Spot the imbalance. Mark a three-candle gap formed by a strong, fast move in that direction.
  3. Wait for the retest. Let price pull back into the gap. Do not chase the move.
  4. Look for a reaction. Watch for a candle that holds the gap and moves back in the trend direction.
  5. Set risk. Place the stop just beyond the gap and target the next high or low.

Inversion fair value gap and the ICT IFVG model

An inversion fair value gap (IFVG) forms when price closes through an existing FVG instead of respecting it. The gap then flips its role. A bullish FVG that price closes below can act as resistance, and a bearish FVG that price closes above can act as support.

The ICT IFVG model gives traders an entry trigger, which is a clear reason to enter. The trigger is the first return to the flipped gap after the close through it.

Step 1

Mark a fair value gap on your chart.

Step 2

Wait for a candle to close fully through the gap.

Step 3

Wait for price to return to the flipped gap and react from the other side. That reaction is the entry trigger.

Rejection block trading setup

A rejection block is the zone covered by a long wick at a swing high or swing low. The wick shows price was pushed away from a level. In a rejection block trading setup, traders mark the wick area and watch for a second reaction when price revisits it.

Bullish rejection

A long lower wick at a swing low. The wick zone may act as support.

Bearish rejection

A long upper wick at a swing high. The wick zone may act as resistance.
